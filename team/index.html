1What it's for

An app for the people who know the problem.

One team lives in one Slack channel and builds one project for the people in it. They don't need to read code: they ask, agree, try, and say whether it works.

A tool nobody would build for you

HR's onboarding checklist, a sales team's price calculator, the report someone makes by hand every Monday. Ask for it in the channel.

@onboarding Late steps get missed. Can they show in red?

Start from someone else's project

Adopt a project from GitHub. The team works on its own copy, never pushes to the original, and a first task makes it run.

$ hi team adopt notes github.com/example/notes

2Try it

A team of your own, one question at a time.

On the Ubuntu machine that will run the team, with a Slack workspace where you may create an app. The keys stay on that machine.

  1. Install hi, if you haven't.

    $curl -fsSL https://hi5.sh/install.sh | sh

  2. Install what a team needs. Then give Hermes an OpenRouter key with hermes setup, and sign in to Claude Code and Codex.

    $hi install hermes claude codex podman restic

  3. Set it up. Seven questions, one at a time: purpose, the Slack app (from a manifest hi prints), channel, people, members, models, and code.

    $hi team new onboarding

  4. Start it, now and at every boot. It answers in its channel in a minute or so.

    $hi team up onboarding

  5. Keep it running while you're logged out. Once per machine.

    $sudo loginctl enable-linger $USER
3How it works

A lead that talks, members that work, and hi in between.

The lead is the only agent that runs all the time. It puts members to work only through hi's broker on the machine, which checks every request before it does it.

{% include diagram.html name="team" %}

Work waits for a yes. The lead proposes what will change and a short list of things to try, and waits. The broker refuses a new task unless the lead names the message that approved it: a reply from one of the team's people, in the same thread.

Every change is reviewed by an agent other than its author. When the coder finishes, the broker has the reviewer check the work and, if it needs changes, hands the notes back to the coder on the same branch, up to three reviews. It refuses a merge that no review approved.

Dev and production. Every merge goes to dev, which people try on a preview link that opens on their phones. Production moves only on an owner's yes, as numbered releases, v1, v2, and so on, each with a snapshot first.

4The details

Keys, releases, the folder, and when things go wrong.

What the person running the team's machine looks after.

Watch it from the machine

$ hi team status onboarding # up, members, tasks $ hi team logs onboarding -f # the lead's log $ hi team channel onboarding # what was said $ hi team say onboarding "…" # tell the lead

hi team say posts in the channel and the lead answers in its thread. It is a go-ahead for the work it asks for, not for a release.

Keys never reach the agents

The boxes hold no keys or sign-ins: hi's proxy adds the model keys on the way out. hi team key onboarding openrouter gives the team an OpenRouter key of its own, so its use is billed and limited apart.

The app's settings go in with hi team env onboarding KEY=VALUE, as environment variables in its boxes only: never in Slack, never in the code. The app calls models at $OPENROUTER_BASE_URL without holding a key.

Releases you can undo

$ hi team snapshots onboarding $ hi team restore onboarding v3 # as it was before v3 $ hi team rollback onboarding v2 # production back on v2

A rollback puts production back with its data as v2 left it; what was entered since is kept in a snapshot. Snapshots are taken daily and before every release, with restic, and hi team backups onboarding --offsite copies them to an S3 bucket.

When something goes wrong

The app's long work reports as jobs. A job that fails or stops moving, production's app stopping, or a failed task opens an incident: the lead handles it, or asks the people, in its thread.

$ hi team jobs onboarding $ hi team incidents onboarding

Members you can change

"coder": { "agent": "claude", "model": "opus", "bundle": "web", "network": "dev" }

Edit members in team.json and restart. The lead is always Hermes, and the reviewer must be a different agent or model from the coder. Each role keeps its own ROLE.md and LEARNED.md, what its earlier tasks taught it.

What the team won't do

Start work without a yes, merge what no review approved, or release without an owner. Nothing the lead asks for widens a member's box, and no GitHub credential enters one: hi pushes from the machine.

At most five ideas are open at once, so work can't pile up beyond what anyone can follow in Slack.

Under the hood: the team's folder

Everything a team knows and has made is in one folder, team-<name>. The containers are rebuilt from it on every start, so a copy of the folder is a copy of the team: stop it, move the folder, and hi team up ~/team-onboarding.

team.json holds the purpose, channel, owners, and members; secrets.json the Slack tokens, readable only by you. lead/ is the lead's Hermes home, with SOUL.md, its identity: edit it to change how the lead works. repo/ is the project's git repository, with the specs the lead and the people agreed in docs/specs/.

roles/ has a folder per member's role, tasks/ each task's brief and report, incidents/ what went wrong and what the lead did, channel/ the channel's log and the files people shared, memory/ the lead's long-term memory, and app/ production's data.

hi team up installs a systemd user service, hi-team-<name>, which restarts the team if it stops.
