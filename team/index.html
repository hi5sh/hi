1What it's for

An app for the people who know the problem.

One team lives in one Slack channel and runs one project for the people in it. They don't need to read code: they ask, try the preview, and say whether it works. The developers build it, on your stack, inside your network.

A tool nobody would build for you

HR's onboarding checklist, a sales team's price calculator, the report someone makes by hand every Monday. Ask for it in the channel.

@onboarding Late steps get missed. Can they show in red?

Start on your stack

A team starts from a template: hi's blank app, or your developers' own, which extends it with your database, toolchain, checks, and the rules agents follow. Your hi server hands it to every machine.

$ hi server templates add acme \ https://git.example.com/platform/templates

Bring a prototype home

Adopt a project from GitHub, or a folder someone built elsewhere. The team works on its own copy and never pushes to the original; an owner's agent surveys it first.

$ hi team adopt notes github.com/example/notes

2Try it

A team of your own, one question at a time.

On the Ubuntu machine that will run the team, with a Slack workspace where you may create an app. The keys stay on that machine.

  1. Install hi, if you haven't.

    $curl -fsSL https://hi5.sh/install.sh | sh

  2. Install what a team needs. Then give Hermes an OpenRouter key with hermes setup, and sign in to Claude Code and Codex.

    $hi install hermes claude codex podman restic

  3. Set it up. Seven questions, one at a time: purpose, the Slack app (from a manifest hi prints), channel, owners, the agents and their sign-ins, and code, from a blank app or one of your templates; then the dev box's NetBird key.

    $hi team new onboarding

  4. Start it, now and at every boot. It answers in its channel in a minute or so.

    $hi team up onboarding

  5. Keep it running while you're logged out. Once per machine.

    $sudo loginctl enable-linger $USER
3How it works

A lead that helps, owners who build, and hi in between.

The lead is the only agent that runs all the time, in the channel. The owners build in the dev box, whose only way out is hi's service on the machine, which never moves production.

{% include diagram.html name="team" %}

Ideas become tasks. The lead helps people use the app and turns what they ask for, in the channel, a DM, or feedback from the app's helper, into tasks in GitHub, GitLab, or hi's own: it looks at the code and the docs first, talks it through, and files it once they agree. It answers how far something is from the tasks and the versions.

Owners build in the dev box. Start on a task gives it a branch, a worktree, and an agent. herdr keeps a workspace and an agent per piece of work, there whether you attach from a terminal over NetBird or from the dev page in a browser, where the agent's session is a chat, with its questions as cards to tap, and VS Code beside it. Agents ask for a second opinion from each other with hi dev review, put a branch on the preview with production's data, and ship it into dev.

Dev and production. People try dev on a preview link that opens on their phones. Production moves only when an owner clicks Release on the dev page, as numbered releases, v1, v2, and so on, each with a snapshot first, and each one can be rolled back.

Handing it over. An app that becomes critical changes hands, not code. An admin of the machine makes IT's people its owners, and the people who built it stay on as developers or step back. The project is an ordinary git repository from your template, with its history, its specs in docs/specs/, and its tasks in GitHub, GitLab, or hi's own; the whole team is one folder that moves to another machine.

4The details

Keys, releases, the folder, and when things go wrong.

What the person running the team's machine looks after.

Watch it from the machine

$ hi team status onboarding # up, members $ hi team logs onboarding -f # the lead's log $ hi team channel onboarding # what was said $ hi team say onboarding "…" # tell the lead

hi team say posts in the channel and the lead answers in its thread; what it asks to change, the lead files as a task.

Keys never reach the agents

The boxes hold no keys or sign-ins: hi's proxy adds the model keys, and the lead's Slack tokens, on the way out. hi team key onboarding openrouter gives the team an OpenRouter key of its own, so its use is billed and limited apart.

The app's settings go in with hi team env onboarding KEY=VALUE, as environment variables in its boxes only: never in Slack, never in the code. The app calls models at $OPENROUTER_BASE_URL without holding a key.

Releases you can undo

$ hi team snapshots onboarding $ hi team restore onboarding v3 # as it was before v3 $ hi team rollback onboarding v2 # production back on v2

A rollback puts production back with its data as v2 left it; what was entered since is kept in a snapshot. Snapshots are taken daily and before every release, with restic, and hi team backups onboarding --offsite copies them to an S3 bucket.

When something goes wrong

The app's long work reports as jobs. A job that fails or stops moving, or production's app stopping or no longer answering, opens an incident: the lead restarts the app once, or asks the owners, who restart and close it on the dev page.

$ hi team jobs onboarding $ hi team incidents onboarding

Who does what

$ hi team owners onboarding add U07ANNA $ hi team owners onboarding key U07ANNA ~/anna.pub

Owners, whom an admin of the machine adds, decide what reaches production. On the dev page's People table they make others Pro Devs (Chat, terminal, VS Code, and SSH) or Vibe Devs (Chat only), who build without touching production, and contributors, who file bugs as tasks with their name on them.

What the box can't do

The dev box's agents work without asking, but its connection to hi offers only preview, ship, tasks, and the like: no release, rollback, restore, or production setting is there to refuse. No GitHub credential enters it: hi pushes from the machine.

Under the hood: the team's folder

Everything a team knows and has made is in one folder, team-<name>. The containers are rebuilt from it on every start, so a copy of the folder is a copy of the team: stop it, move the folder, and hi team up ~/team-onboarding.

team.json holds the purpose, channel, owners, and members; secrets.json the Slack tokens, readable only by you. lead/ is the lead's Hermes home, with SOUL.md, its identity: edit it to change how the lead works. repo/ is the project's git repository, with its specs in docs/specs/, and dev/ the dev box's home and clone.

tasks/ has a record of each piece of work shipped into dev, tickets/ hi's own tasks, incidents/ what went wrong and what the lead did, channel/ the channel's log and the files people shared, memory/ the lead's long-term memory, and app/ production's data.

hi team up installs a systemd user service, hi-team-<name>, which restarts the team if it stops.
